# tau-vanishing

[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.23373197.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23373197)

**Vanishing of Fourier coefficients from a uniform zero-free half-plane, and an uncertainty principle for involutions
in PGL₂(𝔽_ℓ).**

Write `Z(θ)` for the statement "`ζ_K(s) ≠ 0` for `Re s > θ`, for every number field `K`". Cureton and Thorner state
`Z(1 − 1/6000)` in a preprint (input **(I1)**, hexagon:2610.00200v1, unrefereed), and their Lean development
(input **(I2)**: AxiomMath/DedekindZetaZeroFree, commit `09afbb4c`; proofs generated by AxiomProver) proves
`Z(31/32)`; the constant `31/32` appears so far only in the Lean development, and no human-readable proof of it is
public. This repository contains:

* `paper/note.tex`, `paper/note.pdf`: a mathematical note (preprint, version 1.1.3, unrefereed);
* `TauVanishing/`: a Lean 4 + Mathlib formalisation of the implication in Theorem A, built on the Lean half-plane.
  It is **complete** (no `sorry`) in the form `x^{(1+θ)/2+ε}`, conditional on one explicit hypothesis about mod-`ℓ`
  Galois representations; see [Lean status](#lean-status);
* `computations/`: the scripts behind every finite computation the paper cites, with their output logs; see
  [Computations](#computations);
* `Challenge/`, `comparator.json`: the statement-only challenge file for Lean's Comparator;
* [`AUDITS.md`](AUDITS.md): a summary of the internal review passes and of the changes they led to.

What each statement depends on, and how it is checked (Lean, refereed literature, preprint, paper-only deduction, or
computation), is tabulated in [What is formally checked](#what-is-formally-checked) (paper, Appendix B).

## Results

**Theorem A** (paper §3). Let `f` be a non-CM newform of even weight `k ≥ 2` with trivial character and integer
coefficients. Then `Z(θ)` implies

    #{p ≤ x : a_f(p) = 0}  ≪_f  x^{(1+θ)/2}.

Given (I2) (`θ = 31/32`), `#{p ≤ x : τ(p) = 0} ≪ x^{63/64}`; given (I1) (`θ = 1 − 1/6000`), `≪ x^{1 − 1/12000}`. At
`θ = 1/2` (GRH) this is Serre's `x^{3/4}`. In weight 2 Elkies' unconditional `x^{3/4}` is better, so the statement is
of interest for `k ≥ 4`.

*Status.* Theorem A is the transfer of the standard GRH argument (Serre 1981 §7.3(iii); Zywina 2015 §4, following
Murty–Murty–Saradha 1988) to `Z(θ)`, and we regard it as an immediate consequence of the uniform half-plane.
Murty–Murty–Pujahari (Math. Z. 2023, §7(b)) already remark that a quasi-GRH bound `O(n^θ)` for `#{p ≤ n : τ(p) = 0}`
"is easy to derive following a mild variation in the argument of section 4 of [MMS]". What the note adds is the check
that the uniformity of (I1)/(I2) is what the argument needs, a formulation in `PGL₂(𝔽_ℓ)` that needs no selection
lemma over `ℓ`, and the Lean formalisation.

**Theorem B** (paper §1.2 for the summary, §4 for Theorem B1, Corollary B2, Theorem B3 and Proposition 4.10). The
new ingredient is an exact inequality on `PGL₂(𝔽_ℓ)`: every nonnegative class-function majorant `φ` of the involutions
satisfies

    μ(φ) · λ_A(φ)  ≥  κ_ℓ = ℓ(ℓ²+1)/((ℓ+1)(ℓ²−1))  ≥  1 − 1/ℓ,

where `μ` is the mean and `λ_A` is Bellaïche's Littlewood norm, with equality only for the indicator function. The
three-term lower bound for `λ_A` in terms of `μ` from which it follows is sharp for every value of the mean, with
explicit minimisers (Remark 4.3). With a
companion inequality (Lemma 4.9) it gives lower bounds for three explicitly defined error functionals (paper,
Definition 4.1): single-`ℓ` majorants, single-extension certificates with the Lagarias–Odlyzko error shape (for
fields whose Galois group maps onto `∏ PGL₂(𝔽_ℓ)` with central kernel; for arbitrary fields Remark 4.7 sketches
the same exponent with a loss `(log(|P|+2))^{-1/2}`), and per-prime Selberg sieves with pairwise
error accounting, which include Bellaïche's large sieve. None of these functionals can certify an exponent below
`(1+θ)/2`. These are statements about the functionals as defined, not about all Chebotarev methods: smoothing,
Hecke-shape errors, `ℓ`-adic information, sign-changing or cancelling sieve weights, nonlinear selections of `ℓ` and
non-Galois input (Elkies' CM lifting) are not covered. (The `x^{2/3}` of Bellaïche's arXiv v1 is not in the published
version, which acknowledges two errors in the sieve argument of an earlier version, found by Zywina and later by
L. Devin and the referee.)

| θ | source | exponent `(1+θ)/2` of Theorem A |
|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | GRH | 3/4 (Serre) |
| 7/8 | Hu, *claimed, not used* | 15/16 |
| 31/32 | (I2): Cureton–Thorner, Lean development `DedekindZetaZeroFree` @`09afbb4c` | 63/64 |

## Lean status

The implication of Theorem A (`Z(θ)` ⇒ bound), in the slightly weaker form `#{p ≤ x : a(p) = 0} ≪ x^{(1+θ)/2+ε}` for every `ε > 0`, is verified in
Lean 4 (statements and proofs unchanged since commit `756d8b4`; `#print axioms` shows only `propext`,
`Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`). The proof is
conditional on one explicit hypothesis, `LargeProjectiveImage a N`. It asserts that for every large prime `ℓ` there
is a mod-`ℓ` Galois representation, unramified outside `ℓN`, with arithmetic-Frobenius traces `≡ a(p) mod ℓ` and
projective image `PGL₂(𝔽_ℓ)`. For non-CM newforms with integer coefficients, this is the content of Deligne's
construction together with Ribet–Momose (Swinnerton-Dyer for `Δ`), which is not formalised. Everything else is
proved in Lean from Mathlib, TauCeti and the Cureton–Thorner repository, including Cureton–Thorner's zero-free
half-plane `Re s > 31/32` (Lean development AxiomMath/DedekindZetaZeroFree, commit `09afbb4c`; kernel-checked, no

`#{p ≤ x : τ(p) = 0} ≪ x^{63/64+ε}`, where `τ` is defined by `q∏(1−qⁿ)²⁴` and proved equal to the `q`-expansion

### What H1 assumes, and what the Lean theorem covers

* **H1 assumes, and nothing in Lean proves:** (1) the *existence* of the mod-`ℓ` Galois representations
  (Deligne 1971; Eichler–Shimura for `k = 2`); (2) that they are unramified outside `ℓN`; (3) the Frobenius-trace
  compatibility `tr ρ(Frob_p) ≡ a(p) mod ℓ` for `p ∤ ℓN`; (4) projective image all of `PGL₂(𝔽_ℓ)` for every prime
  `ℓ ≥ ℓ₀`. For `Δ`, (4) is Swinnerton-Dyer 1973, with `ℓ₀ = 692` (image `{g : det g ∈ 𝔽_ℓ^{×11}}` for
  `ℓ ∉ {2, 3, 5, 7, 23, 691}`, which contains `SL₂` and a non-square determinant). For a general non-CM `f` it is
  Ribet 1985 / Momose 1981 (Serre 1972 for `k = 2`), with `det = χ^{k−1}`, `k − 1` odd. Since H1 only concerns
  `ℓ ≥ ℓ₀`, one may take `ℓ₀ > N`, so the primes `ℓ | N` play no role. None of this is formalised.
* **Frobenius convention.** H1 uses Mathlib's *arithmetic* Frobenius (`IsArithFrobAt`: `σ x ≡ x^p mod Q`).
  References on Deligne's representations normalise traces with arithmetic or with geometric Frobenius. Whichever
  convention is used, `ρ̄` or its dual inhabits H1: the dual `ρ̄^∨(g) = ρ̄(g⁻¹)ᵀ` satisfies
  `tr ρ̄^∨(Frob_arith) = tr ρ̄(Frob_geom)`, and has the same projective image.
* **H1 is satisfiable, independently of Deligne.** For a non-CM elliptic curve `E/ℚ` of conductor `N`, with
  `a(p) = p + 1 − #E(𝔽_p)` and `L = ℚ(E[ℓ])`: Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich gives unramified outside `ℓN`, the arithmetic
  Frobenius on `E[ℓ]` has characteristic polynomial `X² − a_p X + p` (no dualising needed), and Serre's open image
  theorem (1972) gives `ρ̄_{E,ℓ}` onto `GL₂(𝔽_ℓ)` for all large `ℓ`. H1 is not vacuous in the other direction
  either: for `a ≡ 0` it fails (by Chebotarev, since `tr 1 = 2 ≠ 0`), as it must.
* **Scope of the formal theorem.** `card_vanishing_le` is about *any* integer sequence `a : ℕ → ℤ` and `N ≠ 0`
  satisfying H1, in *any* weight (including `k = 2`); the word "newform" never appears, and the passage from a
  newform `f` to H1 is the unformalised classical input above.
* **Differences from paper Theorem A.** (a) The formal exponent carries `+ ε`: `x^{(1+θ)/2+ε}` and `x^{63/64+ε}`, not
  the paper's sharp `x^{(1+θ)/2}` and `x^{63/64}`. (b) The hypothesis is `Z(θ)` for *every* number field; the paper
  needs only `Z_{M_ℓ}(θ)`. (c) The constant is purely existential: the paper's explicit dependence `≪ (1−θ)^{-1}` and
  its effectivity are not formalised. (d) The `θ = 1 − 1/6000` instance is not stated (the `31/32` instance
  supersedes it). (e) Theorem B and §5 of the paper are not formalised.
* **Trusted base.** The Lean kernel and toolchain, Mathlib, TauCeti and the Cureton–Thorner repository (including its
  vendored class-field-theory library, FiniteAbelianGlobalReciprocityv2), plus the five trusted definitions below. Cureton–Thorner's `31/32` theorem
  is **not** an input or hypothesis: `DedekindZetaZeroFree.main` is a proved Lean theorem that our proof calls.

The statements are:

```lean

theorem card_vanishing_le (θ : ℝ) (hθ : 1 / 2 ≤ θ) (hθ1 : θ < 1)
    (hZ : ∀ (K : Type) [Field K] [NumberField K], ZeroFreeRight K θ)
    (a : ℕ → ℤ) (N : ℕ) (hN : N ≠ 0) (hH1 : LargeProjectiveImage a N) (ε : ℝ) (hε : 0 < ε) :
    ∃ C : ℝ, ∀ x : ℝ, 2 ≤ x →
      (Nat.card {p : ℕ // p.Prime ∧ (p : ℝ) ≤ x ∧ a p = 0} : ℝ) ≤ C * x ^ ((1 + θ) / 2 + ε)

theorem card_vanishing_le_ct (a : ℕ → ℤ) (N : ℕ) (hN : N ≠ 0) (hH1 : LargeProjectiveImage a N)
    (ε : ℝ) (hε : 0 < ε) :
    ∃ C : ℝ, ∀ x : ℝ, 2 ≤ x →
      (Nat.card {p : ℕ // p.Prime ∧ (p : ℝ) ≤ x ∧ a p = 0} : ℝ) ≤ C * x ^ (63 / 64 + ε)

-- TauVanishing/Delta.lean
theorem card_tau_eq_zero_le (hSD : LargeProjectiveImage ramanujanTau 1) (ε : ℝ) (hε : 0 < ε) :
    ∃ C : ℝ, ∀ x : ℝ, 2 ≤ x →
      (Nat.card {p : ℕ // p.Prime ∧ (p : ℝ) ≤ x ∧ ramanujanTau p = 0} : ℝ) ≤
        C * x ^ (63 / 64 + ε)

    (hε : 0 < ε) :
    ∃ C : ℝ, ∀ x : ℝ, 2 ≤ x →
```

### What you have to trust

The definitions in the statements live in `TauVanishing/Basic.lean` and use only Mathlib:

* `ZeroFreeRight K θ`: `ζ_K` has a holomorphic continuation to `ℂ ∖ {1}` with no zeros in `Re s > θ`. This is
  verbatim the conclusion of `DedekindZetaZeroFree.main` at `θ = 31/32`.
* `LargeProjectiveImage a N` (**hypothesis H1**): for every large prime `ℓ` there is a finite Galois `L/ℚ` and
  `ρ : Gal(L/ℚ) → GL₂(𝔽_ℓ)` such that `L` is unramified outside `ℓN`, every arithmetic Frobenius `σ` at a prime above
  `p ∤ ℓN` (Mathlib's `IsArithFrobAt`) has `tr ρ(σ) ≡ a(p) mod ℓ`, and `Gal(L/ℚ) → PGL₂(𝔽_ℓ)` is onto.
* `ramanujanTau n`: the coefficient of `qⁿ` in `q ∏ (1 − qᵏ)²⁴`. `Delta.lean` proves
  `(qExpansion 1 ModularForm.discriminant).coeff n = ramanujanTau n` for all `n`, where Mathlib's
  `ModularForm.discriminant : ℍ → ℂ` is the function `η(z)²⁴` underlying the level-1, weight-12 cusp form
  `CuspForm.discriminant`.
* `UnramifiedAt K p`: every maximal ideal of `𝓞 K` over `p` has ramification index 1 (Mathlib's
  `Ideal.ramificationIdx`). `TauVanishing/Sanity.lean` proves that this is equivalent to `p ∤ disc K`
  (`unramifiedAt_iff_not_dvd_discr`).

`TauVanishing/Sanity.lean` also checks, by `rfl`, that the Galois action on `𝓞 L` used by `IsArithFrobAt` is the
restriction of the natural action on `L` (`ringOfIntegers_algEquiv_smul_coe`), and that the counted set of primes is
finite (`finite_setOf_prime_le_eq_zero`). None of the three is used by the proofs.

The internal review of the formalisation is summarised in [`AUDITS.md`](AUDITS.md); the paper's Appendix A states the
formal theorem, H1 and the trusted base.

## Computations

The directory [`computations/`](computations/) contains every script behind the finite computations cited in the
paper (Lemma 3.4, Theorem B1 and the sharpness proof of Remark 4.3, Lemma 4.6, which Theorem B3 needs for `ℓ < 61`,
Lemma 4.9, Proposition 4.10, Conjecture 4.12 and Proposition 5.3), the auditor's independent re-implementations, and
their output logs. See [`computations/README.md`](computations/README.md) for what each script checks, how to run it,
and the expected output. They need Python 3 with NumPy, SciPy (HiGHS) and SymPy (and `cypari2` for `τ(p)`);
everything runs in a few minutes. The linear programs are solved in floating point with HiGHS; they are not exact
rational computations, nothing in them is certified, and no proof rests on their values. The exact (integer or
symbolic) computations are marked as such in the table below.
